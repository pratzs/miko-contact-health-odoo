Odoo will store john@@gmail,com without a word of complaint.

An address that cannot receive mail looks completely normal in a contact form. Nothing warns you. The invoice sends, the log says sent, and the customer never hears from you until they ring up asking where their statement went.

Contact Health reads every contact in your database and tells you which addresses are unusable, which ones are shared between two contacts, and what exactly is wrong with each one. It never guesses and it never contacts an outside service.

Find them, fix one, watch it leave the list. Recorded against a real Odoo database, not a mockup.

It tells you what is wrong, not just that something is

Every fault comes with a plain explanation, so whoever is cleaning the data does not have to know the email specification to act on it.

It does not cry wolf

Flagging an address that works is the fastest way to lose your trust in the whole audit, so the checks are deliberately biased against it. These are all valid and none of them is reported:

user+tag@gmail.comPlus addressing
o'brien@example.ieAn apostrophe in the name
first.last@example.co.nzTwo part country domain
x@example.technologyA long modern domain ending
a1@mail-server.sub.example.orgHyphens and subdomains

Proven by the test suite that ships inside the module: eleven awkward but valid addresses produce zero false positives, and fifteen broken ones each return the correct specific reason. Run it yourself with --test-enable --test-tags /miko_contact_health.

Nothing leaves your database

Validation is arithmetic on a string. There is no DNS lookup, no test message, no third party verification service, no API key and no account. Your customer list is never transmitted anywhere, which also means the module works on an Odoo with no outbound internet access at all.

It reads and reports. It never edits a contact on its own. The one action that does write, Tidy Email Addresses, only trims stray spaces and lowercases the domain, and you choose which contacts it runs on. The part before the @ is left exactly as typed, because it is case sensitive and changing it could send mail to a different mailbox.
